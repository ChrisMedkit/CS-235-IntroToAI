How the Model Works

Every number on the Match Replay comes from a simple, well-known tennis model applied to real point-by-point data from the 2023 Wimbledon men's singles. The model only ever looks at points that have already been played, so it can't peek at the future.

1. Winning a point on serve

The server wins most points — about 67% across this dataset. The model tracks, for each player, the share of their own service points they've won so far in the match. Early on there's very little data, so the estimate is pulled toward the tournament average:

p = (serve points won + 20 × 0.673) ÷ (serve points played + 20)

At the first point both players start at 0.673. After 40 service points a player who has won 30 of them is at (30 + 13.46) ÷ 60 ≈ 0.72.

2. From points to games, sets and the match

The model assumes every point is independent: the chance of winning a point depends only on who is serving. That makes it possible to work out the chance of winning a game, a tiebreak, a set and the match from any score, by adding up every way the rest of the match could go.

Small edges grow: a server who wins 60% of points holds serve about 74% of the time, and over a best-of-five match that edge compounds further.

The 2023 rules are built in: a first-to-7 tiebreak at 6–6 in sets one to four, and a first-to-10 tiebreak at 6–6 in the fifth set.

3. What "momentum" means here

After each point the model compares what happened with what it expected. Winning a point you were expected to win adds a little; winning one you were expected to lose (like breaking serve) adds a lot. Older points fade:

momentum = 0.9 × momentum + 0.1 × (result − expected chance)

Above the line favors the first player, below favors the second. In this version momentum is shown, not used: it does not change the win chances.

4. From probability to odds

Odds are shown in decimal form: the total a 1-credit bet pays back if it wins. Fair odds would be 1 ÷ chance, but like a real sportsbook the site adds a 5% house margin, so every price is a little shorter:

odds = 1 ÷ (chance × 1.05)

A 50% chance pays 1.90, not 2.00. Over many bets, that gap is how the house wins. Chances are capped between 2% and 98%, and odds never go below 1.01.

5. Limits of the model

  • Points aren't truly independent — nerves, fatigue and tactics all matter, which is exactly what "momentum" research asks about.
  • It only knows this one tournament: 31 matches, third round onward.
  • It ignores rankings, surface history and head-to-head records.
  • Next-game odds treat the next game as an ordinary service game, even when it turns out to be a tiebreak.
